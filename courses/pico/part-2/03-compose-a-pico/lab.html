Lab · Compose a Pico (Greeting Capsule, steps 6–7)

The lab for this lesson is the shared Greeting Capsule lab under labs/greeting-capsule/. This Module 4 lesson exercises steps 6 and 7 of that lab, which cover composing a minimal Pico and resolving its capability requirement by hand.

Why these two steps

Memo23 §9 step 4 (“Create the first lab”) assigns one end-to-end capsule lab to Part 2, and memo2 §4 D4 fixes the two course terms used here:

  • Step 6 — Compose a minimal Pico and validate it. Produces labs/greeting-capsule/solution/pico.yaml and runs the Pico-shape check against the repository’s existing definitions/pico.yaml. This is the only Pico-shape contract the Academy ships today; no new Pico validator CLI is added.
  • Step 7 — Resolve the capability by hand. Produces labs/greeting-capsule/solution/resolution.md in the shape of upstream fixtures/scenarios/resolve-greeting.yaml. The reference resolver belongs to open-engineering-capsule/source (memo2 §1 D1, §4 D4) under memo2 §5 follow-up (b), which has not been published yet. The step records the hand resolution against the one installed provider — exactly as this lesson’s index teaches.

Where to go

  • Lab index
  • Objectives and prerequisites
  • Walkthrough — follow Step 6 and Step 7 for this lesson.
  • Solution report
  • Step 6 artefact: solution/pico.yaml
  • Step 7 artefact: solution/resolution.md

Steps 1–5 (fetch, define, validate, implement, test) belong to Modules 2 and 3; this Module 4 lesson relies on their artefacts (solution/capsule.yaml, solution/greeting.py, the passing test suite) but does not re-run them.

What you will produce (steps 6–7 only)

  • A minimal composing Pico at solution/pico.yaml that declares one capability requirement, oe.greeting@0.1.0, under spec.capabilities.
  • A valid print from the Pico-shape check (Step 6).
  • A hand resolution report at solution/resolution.md whose outcome / resolution block is byte-equivalent to the upstream fixtures/scenarios/resolve-greeting.yaml expected: block at pinned commit bf071c2c1b57.

What this lab does not do

  • No Academy resolver. The lab records the hand resolution; it does not ship or invoke a resolver (memo2 §4 D4 Consequences for the Academy; memo2 §5 follow-up (b) is pending).
  • No fork of the upstream schema or scenarios. build/upstream/ is populated by solution/fetch-upstream.sh and is listed in the lab-local .gitignore; nothing under it is committed (memo23 §3.2 / memo26 §5).
  • No new Pico validator CLI. Step 6 uses the repository’s existing definitions/pico.yaml via a short Python one-liner. The Academy adds no dedicated validator in this lesson.

Verification quoted from the lab

Step 6 — expected output valid:

cd labs/greeting-capsule
python3 -c 'import yaml, jsonschema; d=yaml.safe_load(open("../../definitions/pico.yaml")); jsonschema.validate(yaml.safe_load(open("solution/pico.yaml")), d["spec"]["schema"]); print("valid")'

Step 7 — compare the outcome / resolution block in solution/resolution.md against the expected: block of build/upstream/fixtures/scenarios/resolve-greeting.yaml. They should be byte-equivalent.
